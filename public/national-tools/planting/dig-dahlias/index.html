Should I dig up my dahlias?

The first frost and the moment the soil freezes are different events. Whether a dahlia can stay in your garden depends on winter cold, wet soil and what you see on the plant.

What matters for this decision

You do not have to wait for the first frost, but you should not wait for the ground to freeze.

When is the right time to dig?

Penn State Extension describes lifting dahlia tubers in cold climates after frost damages the tops, but waiting for the first frost is not essential. The main protection priority is getting susceptible tubers out of the soil before persistent ground freezing. Gently lift the full clump and protect the crown and necks.

What if I live in a warm zone?

In a mild-winter garden with good drainage, in-ground overwintering may be possible, but a USDA hardiness zone does not tell you soil drainage, prolonged wetness or whether a specific winter freeze will kill a tuber. Valuable varieties are safer with backup lifted tubers. Never interpret a warm forecast as proof of winter survival.

Storing tubers

Handle carefully, cure according to local extension guidance, store cool but above freezing, and check for mold or shriveling. Poor drainage and soggy storage conditions can cause rot.

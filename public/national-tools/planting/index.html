Why this is different from a zone calendar

USDA hardiness zones describe extreme winter minimums. They do not tell you when a bean seed can germinate, when lettuce will bolt, or whether a fall broccoli crop has enough time left. This engine starts with local NOAA freeze climatology, then applies crop-specific maturity, cold tolerance, soil-temperature, heat and succession rules.

The current NWS cold forecast is a separate near-term safety check. Climate normals describe risk; weather describes what is coming this week.

Fall is calculated, not tacked on

For frost-limited climates, the last viable fall sowing is worked backward from the local fall cold-risk anchor using the crop's maturity time, a late-season slowdown factor and a safety buffer. Hardy crops are allowed more cold runway than tender crops.

In hot or nearly frost-free climates, the tool switches emphasis: cool-season vegetables become fall/winter crops and summer heat can matter more than frost.

Evidence behind the crop rules

  • NOAA/NCEI 1991–2020 climate normals for freeze-probability interpretation.
  • Purdue Extension HO-186 for vegetable timing and germination soil-temperature ranges.
  • University of Minnesota Extension for fall maturity and cold hardiness.
  • University of Kentucky Extension for succession-planting principles.
  • Nebraska Extension for onion day-length classes.

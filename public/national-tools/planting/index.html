How serious gardeners stretch a season

Repeat small sowings

Lettuce, spinach, radish and other fast crops are usually more useful as repeated small plantings than one large planting. The tool only shows another succession when the next sowing still has enough runway.

Protect the shoulder

Row cover, frost blankets, low tunnels and cold frames can extend cool-season production. The tool uses conservative planning allowances and still respects live cold thresholds.

Watch what closes next

The six-week queue surfaces upcoming last-start dates so a crop does not quietly age out of the season while you are focused on today’s work.

How the plant-today decision works

Season runway, not a calendar square

The engine starts with the crop’s method-specific time to harvest. It then asks how much usable season remains. Tender crops must finish before damaging cold risk; hardy crops receive crop-specific cold tolerance instead of being stopped automatically at the first 32°F event.

Fall is slower

Autumn plantings receive a crop-specific slowdown factor and safety buffer because shortening days and cooling temperatures can lengthen calendar time to harvest.

Protection changes runway, not reality

Row-cover mode adds a conservative one-week planning extension for cool crops; low-tunnel/cold-frame mode adds two weeks. Those are planning assumptions inside broader Extension ranges, not guarantees.

The seed packet wins

Generic maturity data are planning defaults. Cultivar maturity can differ enough to change whether a late planting fits.

What the six-week queue does — and does not — know

The queue projects crop-calendar decisions from NOAA freeze climatology, crop biology, cultivar maturity, selected protection and succession cadence. It does not extend today’s NWS forecast beyond its valid window. A target three weeks from now still needs a fresh weather check when that date approaches.

After the local fall anchor passes, the tool rolls its spring reference into the next calendar year so December and January plans do not lose the upcoming spring.

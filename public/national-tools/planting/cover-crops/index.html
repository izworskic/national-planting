What matters for this decision

Late-fall winter rye is a different decision from late-fall forage radish or buckwheat.

Winter rye can go in later than many cover crops

University of Maryland Extension describes winter rye as a cold-hardy cover crop that can be planted latest in the fall. That does not mean late planting produces a full fall canopy: short days and cold soil can severely limit biomass before winter.

Oats, radish and legumes have different windows

Oats and forage radish need useful fall growth before they are winterkilled. Legumes such as crimson clover and hairy vetch need enough establishment time for reliable nitrogen contributions. Buckwheat is a warm-season cover crop and can be frost-killed, making it a poor late-fall recommendation.

Remember the spring removal plan

Overwintering rye and other cereals require deliberate spring termination. Mature cereal rye residue can temporarily tie up plant-available nitrogen and complicate small-seeded spring crops. Do not sow without deciding when you will remove or manage it.

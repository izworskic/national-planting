What matters for this decision

Tomatoes beginning to color usually have better indoor ripening potential than small immature green fruit.

Green tomatoes are not all the same

University of Minnesota Extension distinguishes fully developed mature-green fruit from immature green fruit. Mature-green tomatoes can often finish ripening after harvest; immature fruit may never gain good flavor. Fruit showing a first blush is the strongest candidate for indoor ripening.

When an NWS forecast approaches freezing, the live cold risk overrides a long-range historical average. A seven-day forecast cannot predict exactly when each tomato will turn red, and 36°F is a conservative frost-risk trigger rather than a hard biological damage threshold.

How to ripen indoors

Pick dry fruit before frost. Keep fruit at room temperature in a single layer, without crowding; light is not necessary. Inspect frequently and remove damaged or rotting fruit. Tomatoes that are too immature may be better used for appropriate green-tomato recipes than relied on for indoor ripening.

Source: University of Minnesota Extension — Growing tomatoes.

Why this decision matters

The thermometer reading comes from you. It is never synthesized from the NWS air forecast. Some corn cultivars, melons and other warm-season crops require higher temperatures than the minimum screen.

Soil temperature beats a warm afternoon

Warm-season direct-seeded vegetables such as beans and cucumbers generally need soil at or above about 60°F. Melons may demand still warmer conditions. Cool-season peas, spinach, lettuce, carrots and radishes can germinate at lower temperatures, but very cold seedbeds slow emergence and increase losses.

Temperature is only one gate: beans and sweet corn can rot in waterlogged soil, and overly dry soil can prevent germination. Our verdict uses your measured seedbed temperature, crop group and observed soil moisture together; an air-temperature API cannot measure your garden soil.

How to measure

Use a soil thermometer at approximately the planting depth, ideally early in the day when soil is coolest. Check several locations and more than one day, especially after cold rain. Confirm variety-specific requirements on the seed packet and check local extension guidance before sowing.

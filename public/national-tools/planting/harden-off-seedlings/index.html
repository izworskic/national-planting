Why this decision matters

Two weeks of progressively longer sheltered outings is a useful pattern, not a guarantee. The NWS grid temperature cannot measure conditions inside a pot or against a sunny wall.

Why hardening off matters

Seedlings raised under lights or indoors have never experienced outdoor ultraviolet radiation, gusty winds and day-to-night temperature changes. Abruptly putting them into full sunlight can scorch leaves and cause wilt or death.

University of Minnesota Extension describes a roughly two-week acclimation: start with a few hours in protected shade, add more time and sun gradually, and bring plants indoors as temperatures drop. A ventilated cold frame can help but still requires attention during cold and sunny periods.

Frost protection comes before the schedule

Warm-season transplants such as tomatoes, peppers and basil are vulnerable to chilling and freezing. Even an established hardening routine does not make them frost-hardy. Cool-season seedlings have different tolerances. If the NWS source is missing or outdated, this engine refuses to label outdoor nights safe.
